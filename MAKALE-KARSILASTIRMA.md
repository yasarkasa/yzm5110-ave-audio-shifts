# AVE literature comparison

**Research question.** How does A+V-att event localization change when audio is shifted earlier or later than video?

## Search and selection

The initial search on 9 November 2026 used “audio-visual event localization AVE temporal misalignment”, “temporal inconsistent audio-visual event localization”, and “audio-visual event localization AVE cross-temporal alignment”. Publisher records, full texts and verified author repositories were used. Tian et al. provide the reproduction target. The other four papers report AVE segment accuracy and introduce contrasting interaction mechanisms.

The selection prioritizes CMAN's explicit temporal-inconsistency question, PSP's cross-time pair propagation, CMBS's suppression of irrelevant evidence, and AVSGN's semantic subgraphs. This is a focused project review. The scope and availability statements reflect the initial search.

## Comparison matrix

Full/weak supervision refers to segment-level versus clip-level training labels. The published results below use segment accuracy. AVE has 4,143 clips from 28 event categories, ten one-second segments per clip and an additional background target. The original split sizes are 3,339/402/402. Protocol and encoder differences limit cross-paper comparisons. [Tian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08842)

## Critical synthesis

Tian et al. demonstrate the value of joint evidence on their original inputs. Their supervised A+V-att score is 72.7%, compared with 59.5% for audio alone and 55.3% for video alone. Removing audio-guided visual attention gives 71.4%. A+V-att first matches audio to visual regions at the same second and then models temporal context with two BiLSTMs. This motivates the hypothesis that shifts can disrupt the initial association while temporal context partly compensates. [Tian et al., Table 1 and Section 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.08842)

CMAN and PSP handle inconsistency through different decisions. CMAN selects how much cross-modal context to use through a sentinel gate. Removing it changes fully supervised accuracy from 77.1% to 76.1%. PSP instead connects highly related audio-visual segments across all time pairs. Removing PSP changes 77.8% to 73.7%, while retaining all connections gives 75.9%. These ablations support selective use of cross-modal evidence within the respective systems. [Xuan et al.](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/5361/5361-13-8586-1-10-20200508.pdf) · [Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00239)

CMBS uses temporal and event-level suppression to remove irrelevant evidence. Removing temporal suppression changes fully supervised accuracy from 79.30% to 77.93%. AVSGN adds class-text semantics and cross-time graphs. Removing its audio-visual subgraph changes 83.1% to 80.3%. Each result measures a component within its own system. Cross-paper differences also include changes in encoders, such as AVSGN's CLIP/CLAP features versus Tian's VGG-based features. [Xia and Zhao](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Xia_Cross-Modal_Background_Suppression_for_Audio-Visual_Event_Localization_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf) · [Liu et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Liu_Audio-Visual_Semantic_Graph_Network_for_Audio-Visual_Event_Localization_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)

The five main tables emphasize overall accuracy and component ablations. A controlled curve separating shift direction, magnitude and boundary masking is the focus of this project. Controlled shifting has precedents, including AV-AND. This project makes a limited claim about the published A+V-att weights, original labels, matched boundary controls and paired uncertainty estimates. [AV-AND](https://github.com/cataug/AV-AND#temporal-misalignment)

All paper scores above are published findings. The project's measured results are separately reported in [the test analysis](results/ave_test_report.md). The reproduction target is Tian et al.'s Table 1 A+V-att score of 72.7%.
